# Cue lights and the panic button

Two calls that go to people rather than to a channel. A **cue light** is the
silent standby and GO a stage manager gives a pyro operator, a CO2 op, a
walk-on or playback when the stage is too loud to talk. The **panic button**
is the other direction: somebody working alone holds it, and every phone on
site rings.

## Calling a cue

**Cue lights** in the sidebar. Choose **Who**, type the **Cue** ("Pyro 1",
"Walk-on", "VT"), and, if the running order has stages, pick one. Then
**Call standby**.

The operator's whole screen turns amber and says **Standby**, with your name
and the cue, whatever they were looking at. Their phone buzzes twice. When
they press **Standing by**, your lamp says so, and **GO** is one press. Their
screen turns green and says **GO**.

- **GO before they answer** asks first. The button turns red and says
  **GO without standby?**; press it again within three seconds to send it.
  An operator who hasn't answered may not have seen it.
- **Stand down** calls a cue off. The operator's screen clears and their
  notification goes.
- **Can't stand by** is the operator's way of saying no. Your lamp says they
  couldn't.
- Anybody can press GO or stand a cue down, not only whoever called it,
  because the caller's phone is the one that dies at the wrong moment. The
  history says who did.

Picking a stage puts what is on it now beside the cue ("Main Stage, during
The Hollows"), and that stays in the history after the running order moves.

Below the live cues, **Earlier** lists the night's cues: when each went and
how long the operator took to answer.

### On a locked phone

A standby reaches a locked phone as a notification, and GO replaces it, so
there's only ever one. On Android it's on its own **Cue lights** channel,
which vibrates and makes no sound, because a phone that rings in a wing is
the wrong noise. You can give it a sound in Android's settings for the app.
On an iPhone it is Time Sensitive and makes the usual sound. Either way,
open the app to answer: there's no answer button on the notification yet.

A phone that comes back onto the Wi-Fi while a standby is waiting for it
rings then. A standby nobody finishes is stood down after 30 minutes.

### Without the box

A cue needs the box. With no connection, **Call standby** is greyed out and
says so: a standby is never queued to arrive late, because a cue that turns
up late is worse than one that never came. Use your radio.

## The panic button

**Hold for help** is at the bottom of the sidebar, above your name. Hold it
for two seconds. The red fill shows the hold counting; let go early and
nothing is sent.

Then:

- Every other phone signed in to the box rings, through silent and Do Not
  Disturb on Android (the **Calls for help** channel, on the alarm stream,
  as show stops are), and as a Time Sensitive alert on an iPhone.
- A red bar at the top of every screen says who needs help and asks
  everyone else **I'm going**. Each person who says so is shown to
  everyone, so three people don't all run and nobody assumes someone else
  went.
- Your phone records five seconds of what you say next and posts it in
  **#general**. Say where you are. If the microphone isn't allowed, the call
  still goes, without the recording.
- **#general** gets a line for the call, for each person who goes, and for
  when it ends, so the record is there for anyone who missed it.

**I'm OK** (yours) or **Stand down** (anyone's) ends it, after a second
press within three seconds, and takes it off every phone. A call nobody
stands down lapses after two hours.

Until departments exist, a call for help rings everyone on the box. It is
not narrowed to a channel, because a muted channel must never swallow one.

## Things to know

- Both need the box to be on crewbox 1.5 or later. On an older box the pane
  says so and the panic button isn't shown.
- Both are live, not kept: a box that restarts loses a standby the way a cue
  light loses power, visibly, and the caller calls it again. The record of a
  call for help is in #general and survives.
- The panic button is not a replacement for the site's emergency plan or
  for calling the emergency services. It gets the crew around you moving.
